BIO 130 - Lecture 14 notes: The electron transport chain (ETC)

Where it happens
- The ETC is the last stage of aerobic cellular respiration.
- In eukaryotes it sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane. The folds of that membrane (cristae) give it a lot of surface area.
- Before the ETC: glycolysis (cytoplasm) and the Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix) load the electron carriers NADH and FADH2.

The chain itself
- Four protein complexes, I to IV, plus two mobile carriers: ubiquinone (coenzyme Q) and cytochrome c.
- NADH drops its electrons off at Complex I. FADH2 drops its electrons off at Complex II, so it skips Complex I.
- Electrons move from carrier to carrier in a series of redox reactions, each step releasing a little energy.
- Complexes I, III and IV use that energy to pump protons (H+) from the matrix into the intermembrane space.
- Complex II does not pump protons. This is one reason FADH2 yields less ATP than NADH.

Oxygen's job
- Oxygen is the final electron acceptor. At Complex IV it takes the electrons and combines with protons to form water.
- No oxygen means nowhere for the electrons to go: the chain backs up, NADH cannot be recycled, and most ATP production stops.
- Cyanide blocks Complex IV, which is why it is so deadly.

Making ATP (chemiosmosis)
- Pumping protons builds an electrochemical gradient across the inner membrane (the proton-motive force).
- Protons flow back into the matrix through ATP synthase, which works like a turbine.
- ATP synthase uses that flow to join ADP and phosphate into ATP. This is called oxidative phosphorylation.
- Oxidative phosphorylation makes most of the ATP from one glucose: roughly 26 to 28 of the 30 to 32 total.

Things to remember for the midterm
- The ETC itself does not make ATP. It builds the proton gradient; ATP synthase makes the ATP.
- Electron carriers: NADH and FADH2 in, NAD+ and FAD out.
- Final electron acceptor: oxygen. Product: water.
- Uncouplers (like the protein thermogenin in brown fat) let protons leak back without passing through ATP synthase, so the energy is released as heat instead of ATP.
